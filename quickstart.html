Rural horror storytelling game

English Eerie

A practical quickstart for solo or group play: setup, scene flow, rolls, Story Deck meanings, escalating tension, and how the tale ends.

The goal is not to “win”. The goal is to create a compelling, unsettling horror story—even when the protagonist’s fate is dreadful.

What kind of game is this?

Page 12

You tell the story of one protagonist caught in a tale of English rural horror. They investigate a mystery while people, landscape, circumstance, and something uncanny close in around them.

Solo players write the story in a journal. Groups narrate the story aloud together, taking turns as Active Narrator.

Core idea

Draw a card → turn it into a connected scene → roll if it is an obstacle → record or narrate the result → draw again.

Each new scene should grow naturally from what happened before. The cards prompt the story; they do not replace it.

Create the protagonist

Pages 12–13

1

Use the scenario as your brief

It usually establishes the protagonist’s broad role, period, or circumstances.

2

Write the essentials

Name, occupation, and a short background.

3

Add hooks for the fiction

Choose three defining features and three fears. Features may be possessions, physical traits, habits, or personal details.

Attributes

Split 10 points between Resolve and Spirit.

Resolve + Spirit = 10
minimum 3 in each

R Resolve is determination, courage, and the ability to rationalise the irrational.

S Spirit is emotional and physical well-being. Low Spirit means horror, doubt, injury, madness, or worse.

Group play: everyone contributes details, but you are all telling the story of the same protagonist.

The Story Deck

Pages 14–15 and 18–19

The deck contains 16 numbered story-element cards and 3 Grey Ladies. Each card becomes one scene.

Clue

Reveals something about the mystery: its nature, location, history, or signs.

No roll by default.

Secondary character harmed

Someone other than the protagonist suffers physical, mental, or uncanny harm.

Invent a fitting victim and consequence.

Secondary character obstructs

Another person actively hinders the protagonist through force, persuasion, deceit, authority, or warning.

Obstacle: roll.

Environment obstructs

The landscape, weather, animals, terrain, accident, or circumstance gets in the way.

Obstacle: roll.

Grey Lady

A major turning point. Use the next entry on the scenario’s Tension Table, then pay the cost.

Tension permanently rises.

Set up the game

Pages 15–16

1

Choose a scenario

Read its synopsis, characters, story elements, Tension Table, and Spirit Table.

2

Build the protagonist

Complete the play sheet and place Spirit and Resolve tokens in front of you.

3

Prepare the Story Deck

Put the Grey Ladies aside. Shuffle the other 16 cards and divide them into piles of 5, 5, and 6.

4

Bury the Grey Ladies

Put one Grey Lady beneath each pile. Stack the piles with the six-card pile at the bottom.

Ordinary playing cards

Use three Queens as the Grey Ladies, plus the 4, 5, 6, and 7 from every suit.

The turn / scene loop

Pages 20–21

Reveal
Place the top card beside the deck on the Narrative Track.
Interpret
Use the scenario’s matching prompts, or invent something suitable.
Resolve
If it is an obstacle, roll a d10 and optionally spend Resolve.
Narrate
Write the scene in your journal or describe it aloud.
Continue
Draw the next card and connect the new scene to the previous one.

Group play

The Active Narrator draws, interprets, rolls, spends Resolve, and has final say. Other players may suggest ideas. Pass the role to the player on the left after each scene.

Time in the fiction

Every two cards represent one day in the journal. The final Grey Lady represents a whole day by itself.

Overcoming obstacles

Page 21

The roll

Roll d10 ≥ card number + Grey Ladies already revealed

Success: the protagonist overcomes the obstacle and gains a beneficial outcome.

Failure: the protagonist is hindered and loses 1 Spirit.

Failure creates a fresh problem; it does not stop the story dead.

Spend Resolve

Spend any number of Resolve points, but choose one timing only:

  • Before rolling: each Resolve gives +2.
  • After rolling: each Resolve gives +1.

You cannot spend Resolve both before and after the same roll.

Example

You reveal an Environment Obstructs 6. Two Grey Ladies are already on the Narrative Track, so the target is 8. You may spend 1 Resolve before rolling for +2, or roll first and spend Resolve afterward for +1 each.

When a Grey Lady appears

Pages 19 and 22

1

Escalate the fiction

Use the next entry on the scenario’s Tension Table, in order.

2

Pay the cost

Either spend 1 Resolve or lose 1 Spirit.

3

Raise all future targets

Every Grey Lady on the Narrative Track adds +1 to obstacle target numbers.

At 0 Spirit, you remain at 0. If a Grey Lady appears and you cannot lose more Spirit, spend Resolve if any remains.

Useful edge cases

Page 21

Duplicate card in a row

You may play it normally. Or hold the duplicate, draw another card now, and play the held card on the next turn.

Not inspired by the scenario prompt?

The listed story elements are suggestions. Invent your own scene, provided it fits the card type and grows from the existing tale.

Ending the scenario

Page 22

When the final Grey Lady is revealed, resolve its scene and cost. Then count the protagonist’s remaining Spirit and consult the scenario’s Spirit Table.

Spirit remains

The ending is generally more hopeful, survivable, or ambiguous.

0 Spirit

The protagonist is doomed: dead, broken, mad, transformed, or subjected to some worse fate.

The Spirit Table is a guide, not a cage. Change the ending when your story clearly demands something else.
